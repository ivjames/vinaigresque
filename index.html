How it works

Mix. Add oil and vinegar. Shake.

One pouch makes about eight servings of fresh dressing. It is dry until you make it, so make only what you need.

  1. Add the mix

    Empty the pouch into a jar or bottle, or take a spoonful for a single salad.

  2. Add oil and vinegar

    Three parts oil to one part vinegar. For a whole pouch, that is ¾ cup oil and ¼ cup vinegar. For one salad, 1½ tablespoons oil and ½ tablespoon vinegar.

    Extra-virgin olive oil and red wine vinegar is the house pairing. Others below.

  3. Shake or whisk

    Shake until it comes together, then dress the salad. It will separate again as it sits. That is what dressing without mustard does, so shake it before each pour.

Why mustard-free

A small mix for a brighter table.

Most vinaigrettes lean on mustard. It holds the oil and vinegar together and it carries a lot of the flavor, which is why so many dressings taste faintly of the same thing.

Vinaigresque leaves it out. What is left is herbs, garlic, onion and pepper doing the work, and a dressing that tastes like the oil and vinegar you chose. Nothing to hold it together, so you shake it. That is the whole trick.

It is a pantry mix, not a bottled dressing: dry until you want it, and fresh every time you make it.

Salads deserve better.

Oil and vinegar

Pair. Make it yours.

The pouch stays the same. The vinegar you reach for is what changes the dressing, and the oil follows the vinegar.

VinegarWhat it doesOil to use
Red wineClassic, robust.Extra-virgin olive oil.
White wine or champagneLighter, brighter.A mild olive oil.
BalsamicSweeter, darker, richer.Extra-virgin olive oil.
Apple ciderFruitier, rounder.A mild olive oil, or avocado oil.
SherryNutty, savory, complex.Extra-virgin olive oil.
RiceClean and light.A neutral oil, or a mild olive oil.
